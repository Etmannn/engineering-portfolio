Custom H‑Pattern Gearbox
for Sim Racing

Objective: To design and build a working five-speed-plus-reverse H-pattern shifter for PC sim racing at a fraction of the cost of a commercial one, and to use the project to learn soldering and microcontroller programming.

Result: A plywood shifter with a wooden gimbal pivot and a balsa 5+R gate. Six microswitches are read by a Raspberry Pi Pico running CircuitPython, which appears to the PC as a USB game controller with one button per gear. It works in Assetto Corsa and Euro Truck Simulator 2 and cost about £20.

Key problem solved: The first 6+R gate jammed in the outer gates because my CAD design treated the panel as a flat shape and ignored the wood's thickness. A tilting lever needs more clearance through a thick panel, so I redesigned the gate as 5+R and cut it from thinner balsa, after which the lever moved freely in every gate.
